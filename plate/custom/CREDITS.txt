InstaSlice star solver and public sky data
=========================================

InstaSlice implements this solver and its index builder in this directory.
It uses four-star sky patterns to estimate plate scale, orientation and sky
position. It checks the result against other stars before passing a WCS
solution to InstaSlice Studio's annotation tools.

The wide-field index covers the whole sky with the bright subset of Gaia DR3
and the Hipparcos 2 supplement, taken from the public measurement table named
below. The mid-depth index covers the whole sky down to G = 9.65 (341,627 stars)
from the same Gaia DR3 and Hipparcos 2 data, for fields of roughly 1.5 to 16
degrees. The narrow-field index holds the same stars in smaller sampling circles, for
fields of roughly 0.5 to 2.5 degrees anywhere on the sky. The deep-sky index covers
the following fields in both hemispheres.
Very narrow images outside these fields can have insufficient catalogue stars.
Centres are ICRS degrees; radius is the angular extent of the stored stars.

Method background: four-star geometric hashing as published by Lang, Hogg,
Mierle, Blanton and Roweis (2010), Astronomical Journal 139, 1782. The
solver in this directory is self-contained and includes no third-party code.

Ultra-wide frames: projection.js and panorama.js combine this solver with the standard map projections (cylindrical and azimuthal families)
and a robust least-squares fit. They are written for InstaSlice and include no third-party code.

The index files contain catalogue astrometry and generated star patterns.
Their data terms are Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 3.0 IGO (see ../DATA-CREDITS.txt).
The original test photographs were used locally for calibration and
verification. Their pixels, metadata and detected stars are absent from
the distributed files.

Test photographs: Claudiu Girleanu thanks Jason Marriott (https://www.instagram.com/unsolicitedspacepix),
Sylvain Villet (https://www.instagram.com/sylvain.villet) and Tom Gleichmann
(https://www.instagram.com/deepsky.fragments), whose photographs were used on his
computer to test and tune the solver. The solver is geometry and statistics, not a trained neural
network: the photographs adjusted its thresholds and search passes. They are not part of the app.
